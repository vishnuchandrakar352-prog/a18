Sock Cushioning, Explained

Turn a cushioned sock inside out and you will see thousands of tiny loops. Those loops, and where they are placed, decide how a sock feels underfoot.

01

Terry loops

Cushioning is usually made with terry: extra yarn knitted into small loops on the inside of the sock. The loops trap air and spring back under pressure, softening each step and wicking moisture away from the skin.

02

Levels of cushioning

Most socks fall into a few broad levels:

  • No cushion: thin and close, for dress shoes and tight fits.
  • Light: a little padding under the foot.
  • Medium: the everyday all-rounder.
  • Maximum: thick terry for long hikes and cold days.
03

Zoned cushioning

Many performance socks only cushion where it matters: the heel, the ball of the foot and sometimes the toes. Leaving the top and arch thin keeps the sock cool and lets the shoe fit properly, while protecting the hardest-working spots.

04

More is not always better

Extra cushion takes up space. In a snug shoe it can squeeze the foot, which causes rubbing and hot spots. Match cushioning to your shoe: thicker socks for roomier boots, thinner socks for close-fitting runners and dress shoes.

05

Cushion wears down

Terry loops flatten with use and washing, so an old sock feels thinner than a new one. Washing inside out on a cool cycle and air drying helps the loops recover and last longer.

pro tip

Try new boots on with the exact socks you will hike in. A cushion level change can feel like half a shoe size.
