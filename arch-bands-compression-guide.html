Arch Bands & Compression

Some socks hug the middle of your foot or squeeze gently up the leg. Here is what those features do, and what they do not.

01

The arch band

An arch band is a section of tighter knitting, often with extra elastane, around the middle of the foot. It holds the sock in place so it does not twist or bunch inside the shoe, which reduces friction and the chance of blisters.

02

Support, gently

An arch band feels supportive because it hugs the foot, but it does not change the shape of your arch the way an insole can. Think of it as a sock that stays where it should, rather than as a medical device.

03

Graduated compression

Compression socks squeeze most at the ankle and less further up the leg.

  • Pressure is usually measured in mmHg.
  • Lighter levels are common for travel and long days on your feet.
  • Higher levels are used medically and should be chosen with a professional.
04

When people wear them

Many people choose light compression for long flights, standing jobs or recovery after exercise because the legs can feel less tired. Fit matters: compression socks should be measured at the ankle and calf, not chosen by shoe size alone.

05

Getting the fit right

A good arch band or compression sock feels firm but never painful. It should not roll, leave deep marks or cause numbness. If it does, choose a larger size or a lighter level, and speak to a health professional for any medical concerns.
